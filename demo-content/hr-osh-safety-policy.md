# Human Resources and Occupational Safety & Health Policy

**Policy owner:** Human Resources and Environment, Health & Safety (EHS)  
**Applies to:** All employees, contractors, visitors, and agency workers  
**Effective date:** 28 October 2026  
**Policy principle:** No production target takes priority over worker safety, dignity, or legal rights.

## 1. Occupational Safety and Health (OSH)

The factory shall identify hazards, provide controls, train workers in a language they understand, and investigate incidents and near misses. Supervisors must stop unsafe work, protect the affected area, and notify EHS without delay. Emergency exits, alarms, fire equipment, first-aid stations, safe drinking water, toilets, lighting, ventilation, and housekeeping must remain available and functional.

## 2. PPE Distribution on Day 1

Before entering a production area on the first day, every worker must receive the PPE required for the assigned task. HR and EHS shall record the worker ID, item, size, issue date, and acknowledgement.

Minimum issue may include:

- Safety shoes or approved protective footwear
- Hairnet and mask in areas where hygiene or fibre control is required
- Cut-resistant gloves for cutting or sharp-tool tasks
- Safety glasses for grinding, trimming, chemical, or splash-risk tasks
- Hearing protection in designated high-noise areas
- Apron, sleeve protection, or other task-specific PPE identified by the risk assessment

Workers must be shown how to wear, clean, store, inspect, and replace PPE. Damaged or missing PPE must be reported to the supervisor or EHS; replacement must not be delayed when the task remains hazardous.

## 3. Worker Rights and Non-Retaliation

Workers have the right to report hazards, injuries, near misses, harassment, wage concerns, or unsafe instructions without fear of dismissal, discrimination, loss of overtime, or other retaliation. Reports may be made to a supervisor, HR, EHS, worker representative, grievance box, or confidential hotline.

Reports must be acknowledged, investigated promptly, and handled confidentially as far as practical. Retaliation is a policy violation and may result in disciplinary action. This approach supports ILO principles on safe and dignified work, freedom of association, and access to remedy.

## 4. Shift Timings

The standard production schedule is:

- **General shift:** 08:00–17:00
- **Meal break:** 13:00–14:00
- **Overtime:** Only with prior authorization, voluntary participation where required, accurate recording, and payment according to Bangladesh law and buyer requirements
- **Night shift:** 22:00–06:00 only for approved operations with transport, lighting, supervision, and additional risk controls

Shift changes must be communicated before the roster is published. Workers must receive legally required weekly rest and breaks.

## 5. Attendance Policy

Workers must clock in and out using the approved attendance system and notify HR or the supervisor before the shift if they will be absent or late. Medical leave, emergency leave, and approved personal leave must follow the published procedure. Supervisors may not alter attendance records to hide overtime, absence, or an incident.

Attendance discipline must be consistent, documented, and free from discrimination or retaliation. Workers may request correction of an inaccurate attendance record through HR.
