# QA Standard Operating Procedure: Broken Stitch Control

**Department:** Quality Assurance (QA)  
**Document type:** Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)  
**Applies to:** Inline, end-line, and final inspection teams  
**Factory context:** Bangladesh ready-made garment production  
**Effective date:** 28 October 2026  

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines the controlled method for identifying, segregating, recording, and escalating garment defects before a piece moves to the next production process or shipment packing.

## 2. AQL Control Standards

Inspectors shall follow the approved sampling plan for the order quantity and record the inspection level, sample size, style, colour, size range, and carton references. Unless a buyer manual specifies a stricter limit:

- **Major defects:** less than **2.5%**
- **Minor defects:** less than **4.0%**
- **Critical defects:** zero tolerance; stop the affected lot and notify the QA Manager immediately.

Any lot exceeding an applicable limit must be placed on hold for root-cause analysis, corrective action, and re-inspection.

## 3. Broken Stitch Definition

A broken stitch is a missing, snapped, or insecure stitch that affects seam strength, garment appearance, or construction integrity. It is normally treated as a major defect unless the approved buyer standard classifies the specific occurrence differently.

## 4. Four-Step Broken Stitch Process

### Step 1 — Tag the Defect

Stop the inspection of the affected piece and attach a red defect tag. Write the garment or bundle reference, defect code `BS-01`, location, inspector initials, and inspection time on the tag. Do not remove the tag until disposition is approved.

### Step 2 — Move to the Rejection Bin

Move the tagged garment immediately to the designated rejection bin or controlled hold area. Keep it physically separated from accepted garments and do not pass it to the next process.

### Step 3 — Record in the System

Enter the defect in the QA system or daily inspection log. Record the style number, purchase order, colour, size, line, bundle, operator, defect location, classification, photograph or evidence reference, and corrective-action owner.

### Step 4 — Notify the Supervisor

Notify the line supervisor or QA supervisor immediately. Escalate repeated broken stitches, a developing line trend, or any concern about seam safety to the QA Manager. The supervisor must assign corrective action and confirm when re-inspection is required.

## 5. Rework and Release

The rework operator shall repair the stitch using the approved thread, stitch type, and machine setting from the technical pack. The inspector must re-inspect the garment after repair. Release is allowed only when the stitch is secure, appearance matches the approved sample, and the system record is updated.

## 6. Records

Retain inspection logs, defect trend summaries, rework records, re-inspection results, and shipment-release approval according to the factory document-retention schedule. Never back-date, delete, or alter an inspection record.
