BUYER COMPLIANCE BRIEF: H&M AND ZARA
=====================================

Document owner: Social Compliance and Sustainability Department
Applies to: Factory management, HR, production, EHS, and subcontractor teams
Industry context: Bangladesh ready-made garment manufacturing
Review date: 28 October 2026

1. H&M SUSTAINABILITY COMMITMENT
---------------------------------

Factory managers must maintain working conditions that support H&M's sustainability expectations and the applicable legal requirements of Bangladesh. The factory shall demonstrate:

- Fair wages: wages and benefits are paid accurately and on time, overtime is calculated correctly, and workers receive legally required leave and holiday payments.
- A safe environment: machine guards, emergency exits, fire protection, first-aid resources, clean drinking water, sanitation, and risk controls are maintained and tested.
- No child labor: age documents are verified during recruitment, personnel files are protected, and no worker below the legal minimum age is employed.
- Respectful treatment: no forced labor, harassment, discrimination, violence, or retaliation is permitted.
- Responsible environmental practices: chemical storage, waste handling, water use, and energy controls follow the factory environmental plan and applicable law.

2. ZARA SUPPLIER CODE OF CONDUCT
---------------------------------

Suppliers supporting Zara must protect worker rights and maintain a safe, lawful workplace. Management shall be prepared to demonstrate:

- Freedom from forced, bonded, or prison labor and respect for lawful freedom of association.
- Recruitment, contracts, wages, working hours, and disciplinary practices that comply with Bangladesh law.
- Health and safety risk assessments for production floors, boiler areas, chemical stores, warehouses, and dormitories where applicable.
- Safe access to emergency exits, fire drills, evacuation maps, alarm systems, and trained emergency teams.
- Appropriate personal protective equipment, safety training, accident reporting, and medical response.
- A transparent grievance process that allows workers to raise concerns without fear of retaliation.
- Controls over subcontractors and homeworking so the same standards apply throughout the supply chain.

3. AUDIT PREPARATION CHECKLIST FOR FACTORY MANAGERS
---------------------------------------------------

[ ] Confirm the current buyer code of conduct and Bangladesh legal register are available at the factory.
[ ] Reconcile the worker list, age documents, contracts, attendance, payroll, overtime, and leave records.
[ ] Verify that wage payments, deductions, and overtime calculations can be traced to bank or payroll records.
[ ] Review fire, electrical, building, chemical, boiler, machine-safety, and first-aid inspection records.
[ ] Complete a floor walk: exits clear, extinguishers accessible, guards installed, aisles unobstructed.
[ ] Confirm PPE issue records and training attendance for new and existing workers.
[ ] Review grievance, incident, accident, corrective-action, and non-retaliation records.
[ ] Verify approved subcontractor list, purchase orders, production records, and authorization documents.
[ ] Prepare translated worker notices and ensure workers can participate in private interviews.
[ ] Close overdue corrective actions and retain objective evidence such as photos, invoices, and training logs.
[ ] Brief supervisors: never coach, threaten, or retaliate against workers during an audit.
[ ] Assign one evidence coordinator and one escort; keep original records available for auditor review.

The checklist is an operational preparation aid and does not replace the current buyer manual, legal advice, or an independent audit.
