# Garment Quality Inspection SOP

**Document owner:** Quality Assurance Department  
**Applies to:** Cutting, sewing, finishing, and final inspection teams  
**Industry:** Bangladesh ready-made garments (RMG) manufacturing  
**Effective date:** 28 October 2026  
**Review cycle:** Annual or after any buyer quality manual revision

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This SOP defines the standard method for inspecting finished garments, classifying defects, recording findings, and controlling non-conforming products before packing or shipment. It applies to inline, end-line, and final random inspections.

## 2. Sampling and AQL Requirements

Inspectors shall use the approved sampling plan for the order quantity and record the sample size, carton references, and inspection level. Unless a buyer-specific manual states a stricter requirement, the factory control limits are:

- **Major defects:** less than **2.5%**
- **Minor defects:** less than **4.0%**
- **Critical defects:** zero tolerance; stop the affected lot and notify the QA Manager immediately.

Any lot that exceeds a limit must be placed on hold for root-cause review, corrective action, and re-inspection.

## 3. Defect Classification Table

| Defect | Classification | Typical example | Inspection action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broken Stitch | Major | Seam strength or garment appearance is compromised by a missing or broken stitch | Tag, segregate, record, and send for repair or rejection review |
| Open Seam | Major | Seam opening is visible or can expand under normal handling | Stop the affected bundle and notify the line supervisor |
| Uneven Stitch | Minor or Major | Stitch density or spacing is inconsistent; classification depends on appearance and construction risk | Record the location and compare against approved sample |
| Needle Mark | Minor or Major | Visible needle hole, pulled yarn, or needle damage on the shell fabric | Segregate; escalate to QA if fabric integrity is affected |
| Oil or dirt stain | Major | Visible stain on the body, collar, cuff, or panel | Hold the garment for cleaning or rejection decision |
| Wrong label or size | Major | Care label, main label, or size ticket does not match the approved specification | Stop packing for the affected style and lot |

## 4. Inspection Procedure

1. **Prepare the station:** Confirm the approved sample, measurement chart, buyer comments, inspection checklist, calibrated tools, and adequate lighting.
2. **Select the sample:** Pull garments according to the approved sampling plan. Record style, color, size range, carton number, and sample quantity.
3. **Inspect systematically:** Check construction, seams, measurements, appearance, trims, labels, workmanship, and cleanliness from left to right and top to bottom.
4. **Tag the defect:** Attach a red defect tag that states the garment ID, defect type, location, inspector initials, and inspection time. Do not remove the tag until disposition is approved.
5. **Move to the rejection bin:** Place the defective garment in the designated rejection bin or controlled hold area. Keep it physically separated from accepted garments.
6. **Record in the system:** Enter the defect classification, photo or reference where available, production line, bundle number, operator, and corrective-action owner in the QA record.
7. **Notify the supervisor:** Inform the line or quality supervisor immediately for a major or repeated defect. Escalate critical defects directly to the QA Manager.
8. **Verify correction:** After repair or corrective action, re-inspect the garment and update the status. Repeated defects require a documented root-cause and preventive-action review.

## 5. Records and Escalation

Maintain inspection records, defect trend charts, re-inspection results, and shipment-release approval according to the factory document-retention schedule. No lot may be released while a critical defect, unresolved major defect trend, or failed re-inspection remains open.
