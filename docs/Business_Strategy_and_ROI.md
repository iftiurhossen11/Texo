# Texo Business Strategy & ROI Plan

## Executive Summary

Texo is a security-first, retrieval-augmented AI knowledge assistant designed for Bangladesh's textile and apparel industry. It helps factories find trusted answers across quality, compliance, HR, safety, and cybersecurity documents while preserving source citations and role-based access control (RBAC).

Bangladesh's garment sector operates under continuous buyer audits, changing compliance requirements, high workforce turnover, and tight operating margins. Texo converts scattered factory knowledge into an accessible, traceable knowledge service. The commercial case is straightforward: a modest monthly subscription can save more than its annual cost through faster document retrieval and more efficient audit preparation, while reducing the operational risk of missed requirements.

## 1. Target Market Segmentation

### Primary: Small and Mid-Size Garment Factories

The primary market is garment factories with approximately 50-500 employees. These factories often face:

- Limited IT budgets and small internal technology teams.
- Frequent buyer, social compliance, quality, and safety audits.
- SOPs, certificates, policies, and buyer requirements stored across paper files, shared drives, and individual employees' knowledge.
- High pressure to respond quickly and accurately to auditors and production teams.

Texo is positioned as a practical, affordable knowledge layer that can be deployed without a large digital-transformation project. A factory can begin with a focused document set, such as QA SOPs, buyer compliance requirements, HR policies, and safety procedures.

### Secondary: Compliance Consultants and Training Institutes

Compliance consultants, factory advisory firms, and training institutes can use Texo to support multiple client factories. A multi-tenant or enterprise arrangement enables them to:

- Organize separate knowledge collections for each client.
- Prepare audit-readiness workshops using client-approved source documents.
- Provide faster answers during training and consulting engagements.
- Reuse general training materials while maintaining client-level access controls.

This segment creates a channel opportunity because consultants already have trusted relationships with factory owners, compliance managers, and HR leaders.

### Tertiary: Large Factory Groups

Large factory groups with multiple production units represent the enterprise opportunity. These buyers may require:

- Central governance with unit-level permissions.
- Single sign-on (SSO) and integration with existing systems.
- Custom retention, audit-log, and deployment policies.
- API access for internal portals and workflow tools.
- Dedicated onboarding and support.

Enterprise deals can expand the average contract value while making Texo a shared knowledge platform across a group of factories.

## 2. Pricing Model: SaaS Subscription

Texo uses a tiered SaaS model that allows a factory to start small and upgrade as usage, users, and compliance needs grow.

| Tier | Price | Included Capacity and Features | Ideal Customer |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Free Trial | $0 for 30 days | Up to 50 documents, 5 users, no credit card required | Evaluation teams and exhibition pilots |
| Startup | $49/month | Up to 500 documents, 5 users, basic RBAC, English PDF export | Small factories beginning digitization |
| Pro | $199/month | Unlimited documents, 20 users, Compliance Gap Finder, Bengali support on the roadmap, advanced analytics | Growing factories with recurring audit pressure |
| Enterprise | Custom pricing | Cloud deployment, SSO, API access, dedicated support, group-level administration, custom security and retention controls | Large factory groups and consulting networks |

The Free Trial reduces adoption friction. Startup provides an accessible entry point for smaller factories, while Pro delivers the strongest value for factories that need broad document coverage and audit-readiness workflows. Enterprise pricing allows Texo to support integration and governance requirements without constraining the standard SaaS tiers.

## 3. ROI Calculation for a Factory: The "Why Buy?" Argument

The following model matches the default Business Value dashboard scenario of **three QA managers**. The underlying per-manager estimate remains $6,600 in annual savings, so the calculator can also show the single-manager baseline when the input is changed to 1.

### Annual Texo Cost

- Texo Pro cost: **$199/month**.
- Annual subscription cost: **$199 × 12 = $2,388/year**.

### Savings from Time Efficiency

Each QA manager saves approximately 156 hours per year by finding SOPs, inspection procedures, and defect-handling instructions faster and reducing repeated document searches. At an estimated labor value of $15/hour, this represents $2,340 per manager per year.

### Savings from Audit Preparation

Each QA manager also contributes to audit-preparation efficiency. Across approximately 3.6 significant audits per year, the combined time saved through faster retrieval and evidence preparation is estimated at $4,260 per manager per year.

### Three-Manager Scenario

- QA managers: **3**.
- Estimated annual savings per manager: **$6,600**.
- Total annual savings: **3 × $6,600 = $19,800/year**.
- Total hours saved: **3 × 156 = 468 hours/year**.

### Net ROI

- Total annual savings: **$19,800**.
- Annual Texo Pro cost: **$2,388**.
- **Net annual benefit: $19,800 - $2,388 = $17,412.**
- **Net ROI: $17,412 ÷ $2,388 × 100 = approximately 729%.**

For comparison, the one-manager baseline is $6,600 in annual savings, producing a $4,212 net benefit and approximately **176% ROI**. This confirms that the interactive calculator is mathematically consistent: it calculates total savings from the number of managers, subtracts the fixed annual Pro subscription cost, and divides the net benefit by that subscription cost.

This calculation excludes the additional value of faster onboarding, reduced duplicated work, better consistency between shifts, and avoided compliance incidents. Those benefits can make the actual business impact higher than the conservative productivity model.

## 4. The Cost of Inaction

The cost of not organizing factory knowledge is potentially much larger than the subscription price.

### Failed Audit or Lost Buyer Order

Missing a required certificate, procedure, or corrective-action record can delay approval or contribute to a failed audit. Losing a single buyer order may cost **$50,000 to $100,000 or more**, depending on order size, season, product category, and the buyer relationship.

Texo does not guarantee audit outcomes, but it helps teams find the approved source material quickly, identify missing evidence earlier, and prepare more consistently.

### Knowledge Loss

Replacing a senior QA manager can cost **$5,000 or more** when recruitment, onboarding, training, productivity loss, and transition time are included. When critical knowledge exists only in an employee's memory, turnover creates operational risk.

Texo preserves approved procedures and institutional knowledge in a searchable vector database with source citations, helping the organization retain and share knowledge across teams.

### Regulatory and Buyer Compliance Risk

Non-compliance with buyer standards, including requirements associated with major international brands such as H&M and Zara, can lead to corrective-action plans, increased audit frequency, suspended orders, or cancelled contracts. In addition to financial loss, repeated findings can damage the factory's reputation and reduce future business opportunities.

## 5. Go-to-Market Strategy

### Phase 1: Exhibition and Market Validation

Use the TEXVERSE 2.0 exhibition to:

- Demonstrate live document upload, retrieval, citations, RBAC, and department-specific questions.
- Collect structured feedback from judges, factory visitors, consultants, and technology partners.
- Capture pilot sign-ups and identify the highest-value use cases.
- Validate willingness to pay for Startup and Pro features.

The exhibition should focus on a concrete before-and-after story: a manager asks a question that previously required searching several files, and Texo returns a grounded answer with a source passage.

### Phase 2: Factory Pilots

Offer three free pilots to small factories in Dhaka and Gazipur. Each pilot should have a defined baseline and success criteria:

- Onboard a controlled set of QA, compliance, HR, and safety documents.
- Track time spent answering recurring questions before and after Texo.
- Measure audit-preparation hours saved.
- Record unanswered questions and missing documents.
- Review citation accuracy with factory subject-matter experts.
- Produce a short ROI report at the end of the pilot.

The pilot should convert successful results into paid Startup or Pro subscriptions and provide evidence for case studies.

### Phase 3: Scale Through Industry Partnerships

Partner with BGMEA and BKMEA for industry-wide webinars, practical training programs, and digital-compliance awareness sessions. Additional partners may include:

- Compliance consulting firms.
- Factory management associations.
- Industrial engineering and HR training providers.
- University innovation and entrepreneurship programs.

### Marketing Channels

- LinkedIn content targeting compliance managers, QA leaders, factory owners, and sustainability professionals.
- Facebook groups used by Bangladesh garment professionals and factory management communities.
- University partnerships for talent development, research, and demonstration events.
- Webinars and workshops focused on audit preparation, knowledge retention, and secure AI adoption.
- Referral partnerships with consultants who can introduce Texo to multiple factories.

## 6. Competitive Advantage: Why Texo Wins

### Local Context

Texo is designed around the operational realities of Bangladesh's garment industry: buyer audits, factory-floor SOPs, compliance certificates, HR policies, safety procedures, and multilingual working environments. It is not positioned as a generic chatbot detached from factory workflows.

### Security-First Architecture

Texo applies role-based access control at the retrieval level, rather than treating permissions as a user-interface feature added after the AI response is generated. This helps ensure that the model receives only the content the requesting user is authorized to access.

### Source Citations

Every grounded answer can be traced to retrieved source passages. This is critical in audit-sensitive environments where a manager must verify the procedure, policy, or requirement behind an answer rather than rely on an unexplained generated response.

### Compliance Gap Finder

The planned Compliance Gap Finder compares buyer requirements with factory certificates, policies, and evidence before an audit. This changes Texo from a passive search tool into a proactive audit-readiness assistant.

### Practical Adoption Path

Factories can begin with a small document collection and a limited number of users. They do not need to replace their existing document-management systems before seeing value. Texo complements current processes and can expand as trust and usage grow.

## 7. Financial Projections: First Year

### Revenue Target

The initial target is **20 paying factories by the end of Year 1**, primarily on the Pro tier.

- Paying factories: 20.
- Monthly Pro subscription: $199.
- Annual revenue: **20 × $199 × 12 = $47,760**.

This is a focused target rather than an assumption of immediate mass adoption. It represents a manageable customer base for onboarding, support, feedback collection, and product refinement.

### Estimated Year 1 Costs

| Cost Category | Estimated Annual Cost |
|---|---:|
| Hosting | $500 |
| Groq API usage | $200 |
| Marketing and events | $2,000 |
| **Total estimated operating cost** | **$2,700** |

These estimates are indicative and should be updated as usage, infrastructure requirements, support staffing, and customer acquisition activity become clearer.

### Net Profit Estimate

- Revenue: $47,760/year.
- Estimated operating costs: $2,700/year.
- **Estimated net profit: approximately $45,060/year**, or approximately **$45,000/year** when rounded.

The projection does not include founder compensation, full-time engineering salaries, taxes, payment-processing fees, legal costs, or a larger support organization. Those items should be added to a formal investor model. Even with additional staffing and operating costs, the projected gross margin demonstrates why a focused vertical SaaS strategy can be attractive.

## Key Metrics for Investors and Buyers

Texo should measure the following metrics during pilots and early commercial deployment:

- Monthly recurring revenue (MRR).
- Number of active factories and active users per factory.
- Trial-to-paid conversion rate.
- Average time saved per recurring question.
- Audit-preparation hours saved per factory.
- Citation acceptance rate from factory reviewers.
- Percentage of questions answered from approved documents.
- Customer retention and expansion from Startup to Pro or Enterprise.
- Cost of acquiring a customer and payback period.

## Closing Investment Case

Texo addresses a specific and expensive operational problem: factories have important knowledge, but it is often difficult to find, verify, and share at the moment it is needed. A secure, citation-backed AI assistant can turn this fragmented knowledge into a practical daily tool.

For the default three-manager factory scenario, the Pro subscription costs $2,388 per year against an estimated $19,800 in direct annual productivity and audit-preparation savings. The resulting $17,412 net benefit demonstrates the operating leverage of a shared knowledge system. The stronger upside comes from risk reduction: avoiding one missed certificate, preserving experienced staff knowledge, and preparing more consistently for buyer scrutiny.

The recommended path is to validate the product through three measurable factory pilots, build reference cases, and scale through trusted industry and consulting partnerships. This gives Texo a credible route from exhibition demonstration to recurring SaaS revenue in Bangladesh's large and compliance-sensitive garment sector.
